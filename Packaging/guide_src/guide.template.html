Quick start

Vocalise-Rider listens to a vocal and moves the volume up and down for you, like an engineer riding a fader. It only changes the volume, never the tone, so the vocal stays clean.

  1. Put it on the vocal track as an effect (the first slot is best, before compression).
  2. Play the vocal for 10 seconds or more. The graph fills in: grey is the original, green is the result.
  3. Drag the purple and blue lines in the graph. Purple stops loud parts sticking out, blue lifts quiet parts. The green line should look flatter than the grey one.
  4. Leave OUTPUT at 0.0 dB. That is unity, meaning no extra volume change. Done.
Want it hands-off? Open the cog (top right) and press AUTO SET after playing the vocal. It picks the target, both lines and the silence level for you.

Tame Peaks and Lower Levels, explained

These are the two lines people ask about most. The dB numbers on them are distances from the Target line, not volume levels.

{{DIAGRAM}}

TAME PEAKS +4.0 dB means: the ceiling sits 4 dB louder than the Target. Anything that rises above this line is pulled back down to it. A smaller number brings the ceiling closer to the Target, which tames more.

LOWER LEVELS −7.0 dB means: the floor sits 7 dB quieter than the Target. Anything that falls below this line is lifted up to it. A smaller number brings the floor closer to the Target, which lifts more.

Example: Target −30 dB, Tame Peaks +4, Lower Levels −7. The ceiling is at −26 dB and the floor at −37 dB. A word at −22 dB is pulled down to −26. A word at −42 dB is lifted to −37. Words between the lines are left alone, unless you raise Leveling in the cog, which also pulls them toward the Target.

These are real volume levels in dB. LOUD PEAKS is how loud the louder parts of the singing typically are, QUIET PARTS is how loud the quieter parts are, and GAP is the distance between them. In this example the gap shrank from 18.6 to 14.3 dB, so the vocal is more even. Smaller gap after = better. Silence between phrases is not counted.

Every control

For each one: what it is for, how to use it, and how it works.

1Tame Peaks line

What it is for
Stops the loudest parts from sticking out.
How to use it
Drag the purple line down to tame more, up to leave loud parts alone.
How it works
Anything louder than this line is turned down until it sits on the line. The most it will ever turn down is 18 dB.

2Lower Levels line

What it is for
Brings up words and phrases that are too quiet.
How to use it
Drag the blue line up to lift more, down to leave quiet parts alone.
How it works
Anything quieter than this line is turned up until it sits on the line, by at most 12 dB. Silence is never lifted (see Silence).

3Target line and Target knob

What it is for
The loudness you are aiming for. It is the centre of the ride.
How to use it
Drag the teal line or turn the Target knob (10). They are the same control. Touching it switches Smart Target off so it stays where you put it. The − and + nudge it by 0.5 dB.
How it works
The vocal is pulled toward this level (as strongly as Leveling allows), and the Tame Peaks and Lower Levels lines move with it.

4Silence line and Silence knob

What it is for
Tells the plugin where singing stops and breath or room noise begins.
How to use it
Set it just above the quiet noise between phrases. Drag the grey line or turn the Silence knob (11).
How it works
Anything under this level counts as silence and is never boosted, so the noise floor and breaths are not pumped up.

5The graph

What it is for
Shows the last 10 seconds of the vocal so you can see what the ride does.
How to use it
Play the vocal, then move lines and knobs. The green trace changes instantly, even when your DAW is stopped. Aim for green flatter than grey.
How it works
Every 10 ms the plugin measures loudness. The green trace is the original plus the volume change your current settings would make.

6The numbers table

What it is for
Tells you in numbers how much more even the vocal became.
How to use it
Compare the grey (before) and green (after) columns. Watch the GAP row: smaller is more even.
How it works
It uses the 90th percentile of the singing for LOUD PEAKS and the 10th percentile for QUIET PARTS, which ignores one-off spikes and dropouts.

7Output fader

What it is for
A plain volume control after the ride, from −24 to +12 dB.
How to use it
Leave it at 0.0 dB (unity) in normal use. It is there to turn the result down if needed. Raising it is allowed, but the goal is unity. Double-click to reset.
How it works
Simple gain applied last, smoothed so moving it never clicks. The small line beside the fader marks 0 dB.

8Peak meters

What it is for
Shows how loud the plugin's output gets, left and right.
How to use it
Keep the peaks out of the red. The red light at the top stays lit if the signal hit the maximum. Click the meter to clear it.
How it works
Shows the highest level of each moment after the Output fader. The grey marker holds the recent peak for about 1.5 seconds, and the number below is the held peak.

9Speed knob

What it is for
How fast the plugin reacts. Low is slow and smooth, high is quick and tight.
How to use it
Slow settings hide the riding. Fast settings catch short peaks but sound more worked. Hover the knob to see the real times.
How it works
One knob sets two times. Loud parts come down in about 160 ms at 0 down to 16 ms at 10. Quiet parts come back up six times slower, which is gentler on the ear.

10Target knob

What it is for
The same control as the teal line (3), as a knob.
How to use it
Turn it, click the number to type a value, or use − / +. With Smart Target on it shows the level being followed.
How it works
See the Target line.

11Silence knob

What it is for
The same control as the grey line (4), as a knob.
How to use it
Turn it, click the number to type a value, or use − / +.
How it works
See the Silence line.

12LIVE MODE / WRITE / READ

What it is for
Lets you record the ride as automation, then edit it and bounce.
How to use it
LIVE: rides while you play, nothing is recorded. WRITE: record the ride (see Recording a ride). READ: plays back the recorded ride.
How it works
In WRITE the plugin moves an automation parameter called Ride (dB). In READ it ignores the audio and applies whatever that parameter says.

13Smart Target, Smart Silence, Protect "S" Sounds

Smart Target
ON: the plugin finds the target itself by following the singer's average loudness (about a 10 second memory). OFF: you set the Target.
Smart Silence
ON: when the singer stops, any boost eases back to 0 so the next phrase starts clean and noise is not turned up. Turn it off only if word endings sound cut short.
Protect "S" Sounds
ON: hissy S, T and breath sounds (mostly above 5 kHz) do not steer the ride, so a harsh S is not pulled down and the next word is not lifted because of it.

Recording a ride (Logic Pro)

This replaces writing a ride, selecting it into volume automation and bouncing.

  1. Insert Vocalise-Rider on the vocal track. Set the mode to WRITE.
  2. On the track, show automation and set the mode to Latch (or Touch). Choose the Ride (dB) parameter.
  3. Press play. The plugin rides the vocal and writes the ride into the automation lane.
  4. Switch the plugin to READ and the track to Read. The recorded ride plays back. Select and edit the points like any volume automation.
  5. Bounce in place when you are happy so the ride is baked into the audio.
Keep Plug-in Delay Compensation on. The plugin looks ahead (30 ms by default) and tells your DAW about that delay.

Advanced (the cog)

Lookahead

What it is for
Puts the ride in place before the word arrives.
How to use it
30 ms suits most vocals. Longer is smoother on fast words. Off reacts after the fact. Choices: Off, 10, 20, 30, 50, 80 ms.
How it works
The audio is delayed by this amount while the level is measured on the undelayed signal. The delay is reported to your DAW as latency.

Leveling (0 to 10)

What it is for
How strongly everything is pulled toward the Target, not just the extremes.
How to use it
0 means only the two lines act. 10 means the whole vocal is pulled to the Target. 4 to 6 is a good start.
How it works
Each moment is moved part of the way to the Target, then limited by the two lines.

AUTO SET

What it is for
Sets Target, both lines and Silence in one click.
How to use it
Play at least 10 seconds of the vocal (a verse and chorus is better), then press AUTO SET.
How it works
It builds a picture of every loudness it heard. Target goes at the typical singing level, the lines at the loud and quiet extremes, and Silence just above the noise between phrases.

Tips and fixes

The graph is empty

Play the vocal. The graph shows the last 10 seconds that passed through the plugin.

It sounds pumpy or obvious

Lower Speed, lower Leveling, and move the purple and blue lines further from the Target.

Breaths or room noise get louder

Raise Silence so it sits just above the noise, and keep Smart Silence on.

S sounds get harsh or ducked

Keep Protect "S" Sounds on. If a de-esser is on the track, put it after the rider.

My vocal is late against other tracks

Turn on Plug-in Delay Compensation in your DAW, or set Lookahead to Off.
